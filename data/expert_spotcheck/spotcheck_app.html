Ontology reference

Maladaptive

Avoidance
Hiding, fleeing, freezing, doing nothing, or disappearing from the dream scene instead of changing it.
Interruption
Ending the dream by waking up, forcing it to stop, or cutting the scene off.
Violent revenge
Rage-driven, excessive, or retaliatory harm beyond proportionate self-protection.
Suppression
Denying, minimizing, numbing, or pushing away emotion in a clearly distressing scene.
Trauma replay
Repeating the nightmare or traumatic event unchanged, with no new mastery action.

Adaptive mastery

Behavioral
Active, goal-directed action: facing, stopping, escaping with agency, or proportionate self-defense.
Social
Seeking, receiving, or creating support from people, animals, or helpful figures.
Environmental
Changing the setting, objects, barriers, routes, light, distance, or dream rules.
Emotional
Acknowledging and regulating emotion, choosing calm/courage, or feeling safe while staying engaged.
Mythical
Using magical, symbolic, supernatural, or imaginative powers to transform the dream.

Neutral

Narrative setting
Scene description or factual dream detail without a safety-relevant action.
Affect expression
Stating fear, sadness, relief, or other emotion without suppressing it or changing the dream.

Boundary rules

  • If safe and unsafe material both appear, choose the unsafe node if a therapist would need to redirect it.
  • Proportionate protection is behavioral mastery; revenge, punishment, or continued harm is violent revenge.
  • Emotional mastery acknowledges emotion; suppression denies, minimizes, or numbs it.
  • Leaving danger inside the dream can be behavioral mastery; waking up or ending the dream is interruption.
